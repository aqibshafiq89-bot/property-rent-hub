Renting your first office in Dubai is tied closely to setting up the company itself, so the order of steps matters. This checklist follows that order. Work through it from the top and you will avoid the most common problem: signing for an office that can't be used for your licence.

Before you look at offices

1. Choose your licensing route

Decide whether your company will be licensed on the mainland by Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) or by a free zone. This decides where you can have your office. A mainland licence needs premises with a lease registered through Ejari. A free zone licence needs workspace that meets that free zone's rules, usually inside the free zone.

2. Confirm what your activity needs

Some business activities need approval from another government body before a licence is issued, and some need particular kinds of premises. Check this with DET or your free zone before you choose an office.

3. Set your budget

Decide what you can spend each year and what the rent should include: furniture, internet, utilities, parking or meeting rooms. How Much Does It Cost to Rent an Office in Dubai? explains what decides the rent.

4. Decide on size and type

Count the desks you will need over the next twelve months. Decide between a furnished private office, a serviced office, a shared desk or an unfurnished unit.

Finding and checking the office

5. Shortlist areas

Match the area to your licence, your clients and your team's commute. Best Areas to Rent an Office in Dubai compares five districts.

6. Check who you are dealing with

  • If a broker is involved, check them in the "Licensed Real Estate Brokers" service in the Dubai Land Department's Dubai REST app.
  • Brokerage adverts need a Dubai Land Department permit, and the permit number must appear on the advert.
  • Ask who owns the office and whether the person signing is the owner or holds a power of attorney.

7. View the office in person

  • Walk the route from the metro or the car park.
  • Check the air conditioning, lighting, internet, washrooms and lifts.
  • For a furnished office, check the condition of the furniture and ask for an inventory.
  • Ask what the rent includes.
  • Ask for written confirmation that Ejari is available for your company and activity.

Signing

8. Read the contract carefully

Dubai's tenancy law, Law No. 26 of 2007, sets several rules worth knowing before you sign:

  • Handover. The landlord must hand over the property in good condition for the use stated in the contract (Article 15).
  • Maintenance. Unless the contract says otherwise, the landlord is responsible for maintenance and for repairing defects that affect your use of the office (Article 16).
  • Notice of changes. If either side does not want to renew, or wants to change the terms, they must give notice at least 90 days before the contract expires, unless you agree otherwise (Article 14).
  • Staying on. If you stay after the lease ends and the landlord does not object, the contract renews on the same terms for the same period or one year, whichever is shorter (Article 6).

Also check the payment schedule, the permitted use, who pays which bills, and the condition you must return the office in.

9. Pay against the contract

Pay only once you have a written contract, and keep a receipt for every payment.

10. Register the lease in Ejari

The landlord, property manager or business centre usually handles registration. Check every detail on the certificate. See What Is Ejari and Why Is It Important for Office Rentals?

Moving in

11. Issue or renew your licence and set up services

Use the registered lease to complete your trade licence, then arrange electricity and water (if they are not included), internet and phone lines.

12. Put the key dates in your calendar

Diarise the lease end date, the date 90 days before it, each rent payment and your licence renewal. That gives you time to negotiate a renewal or plan a move without pressure.

The checklist at a glance

  1. Choose mainland or free zone.
  2. Confirm what your activity needs.
  3. Set your budget.
  4. Decide size and type.
  5. Shortlist areas.
  6. Check the broker, the advert and the owner.
  7. View in person.
  8. Read the contract.
  9. Pay against the contract and keep receipts.
  10. Register Ejari.
  11. Complete your licence and set up services.
  12. Diarise the key dates.

This checklist is general information, not legal advice. Requirements change and vary by licensing authority, so confirm the details that apply to your company.
