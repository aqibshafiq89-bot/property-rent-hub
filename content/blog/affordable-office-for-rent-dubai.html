An affordable office is one whose total yearly cost fits your budget and still does the job: a place your team can reach, that works for your trade licence, and that clients don't mind visiting. The cheapest rent in an advert is only part of that. Here is how to find an office you can actually afford in Dubai.

Start with the full yearly cost

Before you compare offices, write down everything you will pay in the first year. The rent is the biggest line, but it is rarely the only one.

  • Rent. Usually quoted per year. Ask whether the figure is the full annual rent or only a starting rate.
  • VAT. Leases of commercial property in the UAE are subject to VAT at the standard 5% rate, so check whether the rent you are quoted includes it.
  • Ejari registration. The Dubai Land Department lists a total fee of AED 177.75 when a tenancy contract is registered online and AED 220 at a Real Estate Service Trustee Centre.
  • Security deposit. Dubai's tenancy law allows a landlord to take a deposit against the condition of the property and requires it to be refunded, or the balance of it, when the lease ends. The amount is set in your contract.
  • Utilities and internet. Included in many furnished and serviced offices, paid separately in most unfurnished ones.
  • Agency commission. If a broker arranges the lease, ask in writing what you will be charged before you view.
  • Furniture and fit-out. Nothing to budget in a furnished office; often the second-biggest cost in an empty one.

Rent the space you need for the next year

Count the desks you will genuinely use over the next twelve months, then add any storage, a small meeting space if clients visit, and room for one or two more people if you are hiring. A compact private office for two or three people costs far less than a larger unit you only half use, and in a business centre you can often move to a bigger office later without changing buildings.

Compare areas, not just offices

The same size of office can cost very different amounts in different districts. If you want to keep costs down, explore office rental opportunities in areas such as these two:

  • Offices in Port Saeed, Deira, an established commercial area close to Dubai Creek and the airport.
  • Offices in Al Barsha, west Dubai, near Mall of the Emirates and Sheikh Zayed Road.

Actual affordability depends on your budget and on what is available at the time. On Sheikh Zayed Road, in Business Bay and in JLT, rents depend heavily on the tower and the floor.

Look at commuting cost too. All five areas sit on the Dubai Metro Red Line, which keeps travel simple for staff who use the train. If most of your team drives, check parking charges and road tolls on their daily route, because those come out of somebody's pocket every month.

Check exactly what the rent includes

Two offices with the same rent can be very different deals. One might include furniture, internet, electricity and water, cleaning and meeting-room hours; the other might be an empty room with separate bills. Ask each landlord or business centre for a written list of what is included, then compare them on the same basis.

Questions worth asking:

  • Is the rent quoted per year, and does it include VAT?
  • Are electricity, water, air conditioning and internet included?
  • Are meeting rooms included, and for how many hours?
  • Is there a service, chiller or maintenance charge on top?
  • How many payments can the rent be split into?

Make sure the office works for your licence

A low rent is wasted if the office can't be used for your trade licence. A mainland licence from Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism relies on a lease registered through Ejari, while free zones set their own workspace rules for the companies they license. Before paying anything, confirm that the office can be registered for your licence and activity. Our guide What Is Ejari and Why Is It Important for Office Rentals? explains how this works.

Ask about payment terms

Spreading the rent across several payments makes a bigger difference to a small company's cash flow than a small discount. Ask how many payments the landlord accepts and when each one falls due, and get the schedule written into the contract.

Be careful with offers that look too cheap

Rental scams usually follow the same pattern: a rent well below the area, pressure to pay a deposit before a viewing, and an advertiser who can't show who owns the office. A few checks protect you:

  • If you are dealing with a broker, check their registration in the Dubai Land Department's Dubai REST app.
  • Property adverts placed by brokerages need a permit from the Dubai Land Department, and the permit number has to appear on the advert. Many adverts also carry a "Madmoun" QR code that you can scan to check the permit.
  • View the office in person before paying anything.
  • Make sure the person signing for the landlord is the owner or is authorised to act for them.
  • Pay against a written contract and keep receipts for every payment.

A simple way to compare your shortlist

For each office on your shortlist, add up the first-year cost (rent, VAT, Ejari, deposit, any commission, utilities and furniture), then divide by twelve. Put the monthly figures side by side with the commute, the facilities and whether Ejari is confirmed. The best-value office is usually obvious once you see the totals together.
