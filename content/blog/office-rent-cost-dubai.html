There is no standard rent for an office in Dubai. The rent depends on the area, the building, the size, how the office is fitted out and the contract terms, and on top of the rent come VAT, registration, a deposit and running costs. This guide goes through each cost so you can build a realistic budget.

1. The rent

Office rent in Dubai is normally quoted per year. Some listings quote a rate per square foot instead; multiply it by the office size to get the yearly rent, and check whether the size quoted is the usable area or includes a share of common space.

What pushes rent up or down:

  • Location, down to the specific building and floor.
  • Size and layout.
  • Condition: furnished, fitted, or shell and core.
  • Services included, such as internet, utilities, reception and meeting rooms.
  • Contract terms: the length of the lease and the number of payments.

2. VAT

Leases of commercial property in the UAE are taxable at the standard VAT rate of 5%, according to the Ministry of Finance. Always ask whether a quoted rent includes VAT; if it does not, add 5% to the yearly rent when you budget. If your company is registered for VAT, speak to your accountant about whether you can recover it.

3. Ejari registration

A mainland office lease is registered through Ejari, the Dubai Land Department's tenancy registration system. The DLD lists a total fee of AED 177.75 for registering online and AED 220 at a Real Estate Service Trustee Centre. The tenant usually pays unless the contract says otherwise. 4. Security deposit

Dubai's tenancy law allows a landlord to take a security deposit to cover the maintenance of the property when the lease ends, and requires the landlord to refund it, or what remains of it, at the end of the contract. The amount is agreed in the contract, so ask for it at the start and keep the receipt.

5. Utilities, cooling and internet

Dubai's unified tenancy contract puts the electricity, water, cooling and gas bills from your occupation on the tenant. In many furnished and serviced offices, some or all of these are included in the rent instead. For an unfurnished office, budget for:

  • electricity and water connection and monthly bills;
  • cooling charges, which some buildings bill separately from electricity;
  • internet and phone lines.

6. Agency commission

If a broker arranges the lease, a commission may be payable. Ask what it will be, in writing, before the viewing, and pay it only against a receipt from the brokerage.

7. Furniture and fit-out

A furnished office has no fit-out cost. A fitted office needs furniture and equipment. A shell-and-core unit needs a full interior fit-out, which usually requires approvals from the building management and, depending on the area, the relevant authority. Read Furnished vs. Unfurnished Offices in Dubai before you choose.

8. Parking and getting there

Parking is sometimes included and sometimes charged per space. Ask how many bays come with the office, what extra bays cost and where visitors park. If your team drives, daily parking and road tolls add up over a year.

9. Licence costs are separate

The cost of your trade licence is paid to your licensing authority, not your landlord, and depends on your activity and legal form. Mainland licences are issued by Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism; free zone licences by the relevant free zone. Ask a setup adviser or the authority for current figures.

Add up the first-year total for each office you are considering, divide by twelve and compare the monthly figures. That is the number that tells you which office you can afford.
