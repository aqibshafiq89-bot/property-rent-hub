Furnished vs. Unfurnished Offices in Dubai

By the Property Rent Hub teamUpdated 22 November 20263 min read

In Dubai, office listings usually describe a unit in one of three states: furnished, fitted or shell and core. Which one you choose decides how fast you can move in, how much cash you need up front and how much control you have over the space.

  2. Why companies choose a furnished office
  3. Why companies choose an unfurnished office
  4. Which one suits you?

What the terms mean

  • Furnished: ready to use, with desks, chairs and storage in place. Furnished offices in business centres often come with internet, utilities and shared facilities too.
  • Fitted: the interior work is done (partitions, ceilings, lighting, air conditioning and flooring) but there is no furniture.
  • Shell and core: a bare unit. You design and build the interior yourself before you can use it.

"Unfurnished" can mean either fitted or shell and core, so always ask which.

Why companies choose a furnished office

  • You can start straight away. There is no furniture to order, no fit-out to manage and no waiting for deliveries.
  • Less to buy up front. You avoid buying furniture and equipment before you move in.
  • Predictable monthly costs, especially where internet and utilities are included in the rent.
  • Easier to move. If your team outgrows the office, there is no furniture to sell or store.

The trade-offs: you have little say over the layout and the look, furniture quality varies from building to building, and the rent may be higher than for an empty office of the same size because furniture and services are built into it.

Why companies choose an unfurnished office

  • Control. You choose the layout, the furniture and how the space reflects your brand.
  • Long-term value. For a stable team planning to stay several years, owning the furniture can cost less over time.
  • Special requirements. Server rooms, extra storage or a reception area are easier to plan in an empty space.

The trade-offs are time and money. Fit-out work in Dubai generally needs approval from the building management and, depending on the area, from the relevant authority before work starts. Ask the landlord what approvals are needed, how long they usually take, and whether any rent-free period is offered while you fit out.

Which one suits you?

Your situationUsually the better fit
You need to move in within a few weeksFurnished
You are a new company keeping upfront costs lowFurnished
Your team size may change in the next yearFurnished
You plan to stay several years with a stable teamUnfurnished (fitted)
You need a specific layout or a branded spaceUnfurnished (fitted or shell and core)

Either option can work for a mainland trade licence, provided the lease can be registered through Ejari. Confirm that before you sign, whichever type you choose.

What to check when you view a furnished office

  • The condition of desks, chairs and storage. Sit in the chairs.
  • Internet: who provides it, the speed, and whether it is shared with other tenants.
  • Air conditioning, lighting and noise from neighbouring offices.
  • Meeting rooms: whether they are included and how you book them.
  • An inventory list of the furniture, attached to the contract.
  • Who repairs broken items. Dubai's tenancy law makes the landlord responsible for maintenance and for repairing defects that affect your use of the office, unless the contract says otherwise, so read that clause.

What to check when you view an unfurnished office

  • Whether it is fitted or shell and core, and what exactly is already installed.
  • The approvals needed for your fit-out and who applies for them.
  • Whether the landlord offers a rent-free period for fit-out.
  • The condition you must return the office in when the lease ends.
  • Who pays for utilities. Dubai's unified tenancy contract puts the electricity, water, cooling and gas bills from your occupation on the tenant, so check whether your contract or package changes that.
