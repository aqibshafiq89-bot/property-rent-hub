Private Office vs. Shared Workspace in Dubai

By the Property Rent Hub teamUpdated 22 November 20263 min read

A shared workspace gives you a desk among other companies; a private office gives you a room of your own. Both are common in Dubai's business centres, and both can be the right answer. The choice comes down to how you work, who visits you, what your licence needs and how quickly you expect to grow.

What you get with each

Shared workspace. A desk in an open office used by several companies. Some are "hot desks" you take when you arrive; others are dedicated desks kept for you. You share meeting rooms, the kitchen and reception. Some packages are "flexi desks" designed mainly to give a company a registered workspace for its licence.

Private office. A lockable room for your team, usually furnished in a business centre, with access to the same shared facilities. Your files, equipment and conversations stay behind your own door.

When a shared workspace makes sense

  • You work alone, or with one other person.
  • Most of your work happens at client sites or online.
  • You want the lowest fixed cost while the business finds its feet.
  • You value flexibility more than privacy.

When a private office is the better choice

  • Confidential work. If you handle client files, contracts or financial data, a lockable room is far easier to manage.
  • Calls and meetings. A private office lets you take calls and hold short meetings without booking a room every time.
  • A team of two or more. Several people working together need space to talk without disturbing others.
  • Equipment and stock. Samples, documents, printers or monitors are safer behind your own door.
  • Client impressions. Bringing a client to your own office says something different from a hot desk.

Check the licence side first

Whatever you choose, make sure it works for your licence before you pay.

  • Mainland (DET) licences rely on a premises lease registered through Ejari. Many business centres offer desks and offices that come with an Ejari certificate. Ask whether the specific package is accepted for your activity.
  • Free zone licences follow the free zone's own rules. DMCC's guidelines, for example, cap the number of licences that can share one flexi desk lease at three, while companies working from a business centre or a serviced office are treated differently.

Our guide What Is Ejari and Why Is It Important for Office Rentals? explains registration in more detail.

Comparing what you get

Compare the whole package, not just the rent:

  • Is the desk dedicated to you, or first come, first served?
  • How many meeting-room hours are included?
  • Are internet, utilities, printing and refreshments included?
  • Is Ejari included?
  • What are the access hours, and can you work evenings and weekends?
  • How much notice do you need to give to upgrade, downsize or leave?

Plan for growth

Moving your company's address means updating your licence and other records, so think a year ahead. If you start at a shared desk, choose a business centre with private offices in the same building. You can then move to an office when you hire without changing your address or starting again with a new landlord.

Our suggestion

If you are on your own and rarely host clients, start with a shared workspace that is accepted for your licence. If you have a team, handle confidential work or meet clients often, a small furnished private office is usually worth the difference.
