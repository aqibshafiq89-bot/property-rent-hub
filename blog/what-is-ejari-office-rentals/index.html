What Is Ejari and Why Is It Important for Office Rentals?

By the Property Rent Hub teamUpdated 22 November 20264 min read

Ejari, which means "my rent" in Arabic, is Dubai's official system for registering tenancy contracts. It is run by the Dubai Land Department (DLD) and its regulatory arm, the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA). If you rent an office in mainland Dubai, the lease has to be registered in Ejari, and you will need that registration for your trade licence.

What Ejari does

Ejari puts your tenancy contract on an official government record: who the landlord and tenant are, which unit is rented, the rent and the dates. Dubai's tenancy law, Law No. 26 of 2007, says lease contracts are registered with RERA, and it adds that courts and government bodies will not hear a dispute about a lease unless the contract is registered. Registration protects both sides.

Why it matters when you rent an office

  • Your trade licence. A mainland trade licence from Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) relies on a registered lease for your business premises, both when the licence is issued and each time it is renewed.
  • Utilities. Electricity and water connections for the office use your Ejari details.
  • Visas and other services. Government services often ask for a registered tenancy contract.
  • Disputes. If something goes wrong with the landlord, the registered contract is what the rental dispute process works from.

Who registers it

Dubai's unified tenancy contract states that the landlord undertakes to register the contract in Ejari and provide the documents needed. In practice the landlord, the property management company or the business centre often handles it. Agree who will do it, and when, before you sign.

How to register

The DLD offers four channels:

  • the DLD website (the Ejari system);
  • the Dubai REST app;
  • the Dubai Now app;
  • Real Estate Service Trustee Centres across Dubai.

Not every channel suits every lease. The DLD's terms for the mobile apps describe tenants and landlords who are individuals, and companies registering online need a property management licence. A company renting an office will therefore usually register through its landlord or property manager, or at a Trustee Centre. A centre visit takes about 25 minutes of processing, according to the DLD, plus any waiting time.

Documents you will need

Requirements depend on the channel and the property, but for an office lease expect:

  • the signed unified tenancy contract;
  • your company's trade licence;
  • passport, visa and Emirates ID copies for the person signing for the company;
  • the landlord's ID or passport copy and the title deed details;
  • a power of attorney if someone is applying on behalf of the landlord or tenant;
  • for renewals, the previous Ejari and a recent electricity and water bill.

Renewing and cancelling

Ejari follows the contract. When you renew the lease, the Ejari registration is renewed too, usually every year. When you move out, the registration is cancelled so the unit can be registered to the next tenant, and so your licence is not tied to an office you no longer use.

Free zones work differently

Free zones license companies under their own rules, including rules on what workspace a company needs. DMCC, the free zone that covers Jumeirah Lakes Towers, is one example: its guidelines set out which lease documents its member companies must provide for different kinds of workspace. Whether a free-zone office also uses Ejari depends on the authority and the building, so check with the authority that licenses your company before you sign.

Ejari with business centres, desks and virtual offices

Many business centres in Dubai offer private offices, desks or "virtual office" packages that come with an Ejari certificate. These can be a practical way to meet a premises requirement. The important question is whether the package is accepted for your specific licence and activity, so confirm that before you pay.

Before you sign an office lease

  • Confirm in writing that the office can be registered through Ejari in your company's name.
  • Check that names, licence number, unit number, rent and dates on the contract are correct; mismatches are a common cause of delays.
  • Agree who registers the contract, and when.
  • Keep the Ejari certificate with your licence documents, and diarise the renewal date.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm current requirements with the Dubai Land Department and your licensing authority.
