How to Choose the Right Office Location for Your Business

By the Property Rent Hub teamUpdated 22 November 20264 min read

An office location shapes your daily life for at least a year: how long your team spends commuting, how easily clients find you, and what your address says about your business. Choosing well comes down to a handful of questions, asked in the right order.

1. Start with your licence

In Dubai, your licence narrows where you can be. A mainland company licensed by the Department of Economy and Tourism needs an office whose lease can be registered through Ejari, which rules in areas such as Port Saeed, Al Barsha and Business Bay. A company licensed by a free zone generally needs workspace that meets that free zone's rules; a DMCC company, for instance, will usually look at Jumeirah Lakes Towers.

Settle this first. It saves you from falling for an office you can't use.

2. Map your clients and your team

Write down where your main clients are and where each member of your team lives. Then look at the journeys:

  • By metro. The Red Line runs from Deira along Sheikh Zayed Road to JLT and beyond, and the Green Line covers much of old Dubai. An office near a station on the right line can cut commutes dramatically.
  • By car. Look at the main roads your team uses, the Salik toll gates on the way, and traffic at the times they travel.
  • For clients. If clients visit you, choose an address that is easy to find and easy to park at.

3. Check access on the ground

Maps hide the details that matter day to day. Visit at the time your team would arrive and check:

  • The walk from the metro or bus stop, and whether it is shaded. A short walk in winter can be a hard one in summer.
  • Parking for staff and visitors, and what it costs.
  • How cars reach the building, especially on a highway such as Sheikh Zayed Road where a U-turn can add minutes.
  • Building access hours, security and reception.

4. Think about the address your clients will see

Your office address appears on your licence, invoices, website and business cards. A tower on Sheikh Zayed Road or in Business Bay sends a different message from an office near a souk in Deira, and both can be right: a trading company may want to be where its suppliers and customers already are. Choose the address that fits how your clients see you.

5. Look at the whole location, not just the office

An office is only as good as the journey to it. For each shortlisted area, think about parking and your team's daily commute. An office near the metro can suit the business better than one that everyone has to drive to.

6. Look twelve to twenty-four months ahead

Changing your office means changing your registered address, so it is worth planning ahead. If you expect to hire, choose a building or business centre with bigger offices available, so you can move up without changing area.

A simple scoring method

Give each shortlisted location a score from 1 to 5 for each factor, multiply by the weight that reflects how much it matters to you, and add up the totals. Adjust the weights to suit your business; these are only a starting point.

Client access3Most clients within a short drive or metro ride

Treat licence fit as a pass or fail: if an office fails it, drop it from the list whatever its other scores.

The five areas we cover

All five sit on the Dubai Metro Red Line:

  • Port Saeed: Deira, by Dubai Creek and close to the airport.
  • Sheikh Zayed Road: Dubai's main business road.
  • Business Bay: next to Downtown Dubai.
  • Al Barsha: west Dubai, near Mall of the Emirates.
  • Jumeirah Lakes Towers: lakeside towers in the DMCC free zone.
